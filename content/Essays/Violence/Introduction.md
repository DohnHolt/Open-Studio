It was the steepest hill I had ever climbed. My legs were warmed, my shoulders burned; my toes were curled, my fingers clawed. The bigger, bolder boys bounded up behind, bouncing past me, braving the bouldering course as I braced myself, cautiously clinging to the multi-coloured climbing holds, carressing the safety of the countours of the hill as others shot straight up the centre. The fibreglass was both safety in my hand, but fear in my mind, for one slip, one giving off my grip, and I would bounce off them as I bruised my way back down to basecamp and terra firma. At the time this was my Everest, and the reward was a fully enclosed, exceptionally fast, tornado twisted tube slide. Twenty five years later, when I serendipitously lived two miles down the road from my firs house for a few months, I returned, daughter in tow, and in between sessions of spotting her from behind I ran up this slight incline in a few seconds flat.
