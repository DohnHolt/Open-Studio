Richard Parry (2003) writes that Aristotle believed technē aims for good and forms an end, which could be the activity itself or a product formed from the activity.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techne#cite_note-:4-3) Aristotle used health as an example of an end that is produced from the techne of medicine. To make a distinction between technē and [arete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arete "Arete"), he said the value of technē is the end product while arete values choosing the action that promotes the best moral good.
