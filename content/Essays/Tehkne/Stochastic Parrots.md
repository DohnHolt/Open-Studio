All the panelists stressed the importance of transparency of the source data, which is hard to document for Large Language Models. **"Just because a data set is big doesn’t mean it’s representative** **and we want people to know that data sets can lie about reality and have biases,”** explained Dr. Bender, as the Stochastic Parrots paper was largely addressing this issue.

Dr. Timnit Gebru explained that the questions the Institute is raising around LLMS don’t question the technology itself (as opposed to [longtermist ideologues](https://www.currentaffairs.org/2021/07/the-dangerous-ideas-of-longtermism-and-existential-risk) and self-proclaimed experts who think AI is bound to destroy us all) **but rather how they are built, marketed, and used**. “LLMs are marketed as replacing humans, and we have to ask ourselves **who is exploited in the process**, and how it’s put out in the market.”

**Scale down and engage vs scale up and disrupt!” someone in the chat summarized.**
