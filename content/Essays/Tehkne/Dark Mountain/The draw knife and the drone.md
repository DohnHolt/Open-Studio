This book is not a shrill screed against the ills of the modern
techno-cult, but rather a meditation on the enormity of this
moment for humanity. Both those who fight for unrestrained tech-
nological development and those who rail against it in toto fall foul
of shallow thinking: is the scythe the same as the Scanning
Tunnelling Microscope? The draw-knife the same as the drone? Or
the hand axe the same as the atom bomb? We give up too much
contemplative breadth when we presuppose that they are. Human
life needs reframing, recrafting, reweaving; a surrogate, virtual,
second life, sacrificed on the altar of the technological gods, is no
substitute for a life of immersion in rich, storied objects and rela-
tionships. We hope this anthology goes some way towards giving
space to these possibilities.
