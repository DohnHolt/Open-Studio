How do you unschool mathematics? I guess firstly knowing what unschooling is is a good start. One definition that I have been playing around with in my mind is this: creative solutions that everyone can consent to that helps move people on along the path of flourishing. 

The problem of school is that it does move people along the "path of flourishing", but that path is very narrow because the ends are predetermined, therefore the path is predetermined because actually is is what it means to be flourishing that is predetermined, and actually almost no-one is happy, no-one feels like they consent to this arrangement, young people especially, but teachers also, who lament that as the curriculum becomes narrower and narrower and more prescribed, they are no longer able to exercise their creative capacities to teach and are just knowledge delivery units. 

When I said the path to flourishing earlier I meant the broad path to flourishing as a whole human being. But that lens can be narrowed down, life is generally fractal, to start using some mathematical terminology, and so what does it mean to help young people flourish as mathematicians in an unschooling family? 

Maths is an interesting subject for a variety of reasons. If we take another one first, say history, the options for learning that are open to us as opposed to the national curriculum are vast. The great expanse of history is literally too large to learn in a lifetime and there are so many different historical periods, perspectives and places that the curriculum does not touch, and so many different avenues into this rich body of knowledge. I think parents can easily see the disparity between the narrowness of the whitewashed Eurocentric history of Empire that is taught in schools and the actual capacity for learning about the Ming Dynasty, Genghis Khan, the initial spread of Buddhism, the Silk Road trading posts in Persia, about black, indigenous, peasant or even hyper-local histories. 

What we have here is one part of the unschooling definition; the flourishing part. Access to this vast knowledge body is what allows people to flourish. Competency creates agency. In the arena of learning history collating knowledge is a large part of competency, and therefore immersing yourself in the knowledge body of whatever self-directed history interests you have will create more agency, in a positive feedback cycle. 

I think parents can see this, and furthermore I think parents can see easily how they can come up with creative solutions to help facilitate access to history "I'm sure this must be other YouTube videos on this", "When we go to the library let's look for books on this", "What a great suggestion, I wonder where you can find out more about that?", "I found a museum/art gallery with an exhibit which is related to [current obsession], do you want to go?", "I see you are enjoying reading about Japanese samurai, shall we try drawing our own characters". 

There are so many ways into the body of knowledge that can take such winding paths. WE could help connect a young person's interests in say pokemon to Japanese art more broadly and that could lead us into Japanese history. An interest in having the mythology of Ancient Greece read to them as picture books as a young child might lead into the actual broader history of Ancient Greece and Alexander's empire as they grow older. These examples are not meant to exemplify preplanned ideas by parents that can get young people to learn history, but as potential sites for exploration that might occur. What I am trying to demonstrate hopefully is that none of these should seem daunting; that reading Greek myths in picture books before moving on to chapter book versions of the same myths over time and then visiting the library and seeing that you have read all the Greek myths twice over, but "hey, what about this book about  the history of Ancient Greece" is not a facilitation that should create panic in your capacities as a parent.

So let's turn now to mathematics. And let us welcome in the panic. 

I once sat around a friend's dinner table talking to them about maths. They were asking the kind of questions this essay is pointing towards: so how do you get your children into maths and support them on that journey? It transpired that both parents had hated maths at school and had the commonly self-diagnosed maths anxiety. In a bid to not pass that on to their children they had very cleverly not allowed that anxiety to be present in their household,but sadly their anxiety was so powerfully strong that meant avoiding most maths completely, creating a situation where maths was implicitly taboo and, in a bid not to pass on maths anxiety, they had encultured their children into having maths phobia. 

As I said maths is an interesting subject. It's no wonder that people are so keen to ask questions about it in ways that they don't about other subjects. I work with unschoolers, oftentimes they are people who have never been to school, I have ran specific maths courses just for people who are homeschooled and the interesting thing is that, if it was a 50-50 split, you would be hard pressed to guess which teenagers have never been to school and which ones did all of Primary and then left secondary school just based on their relationship with mathematics if 



In maths, the curriculum can be broadened. Natural math and the idea of calculus for five year olds is something that I actually advocate for. But also finding ways into the curriculum that currently exists from real life is also an option.

Maths is interesting because of cultural baggage. Maths anxiety, can lead to math phobia if you put of maths and hope that they might unschool it on their own. They might eventually but avoiding the message of actively being scared by not mentioning it all can provide the message of being scared by virtue of never tackling it. 

If you are maths anxious the far easiest goal is to focus on ways in which you can access the Primary national curriculum through life itself as the maths that is involved in this is not complicated for an adult to understand and if you need to revisit it to create familiarity I strongly believe that you can do that.
