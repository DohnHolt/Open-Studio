Apprenticeships are common in [[Indigineous pedagogies]] in facilitating craftmanship: learning to weave, learning to make bows and arrows, making pottery, blacksmithing; and learning the role of the shaman: how to manufacture the spirit masks for ceremonial dances, or the initiation rituals. 

Often in such societies apprentices are expected to reach some level of competency by [[Learning through observation]] and instruction is often explicitly not given as [[didactic teaching is rare]] in such societies. In such societies, for the most part, the educational philosophy emphasises an [[open book approach to learning]] and the emphasis is on the child [[self-directing their learning]]. 

However, apprenticeships are often the one part of indigenous cultures where didactic teaching is most common. It is the act of becoming an apprentice which signals that you are willing to become moulded by an expert and it is at that point therefore that explicit instruction is given.

One question that looms today is can we bring back the apprenticeship model of learning using digital technology. As [Henrik Karlsson](https://escapingflatland.substack.com/p/apprenticeship-online) notes it might be possible for us to [[Create apprenticeships that are scalable]] utilising digital tools can transform the educational landscape. Over a hundred years ago the Victorian school designers had a choice of apprenticeships, which were effective but not scalable, or school, which was scalable but not effective. We can now make another choice though and choose to create apprenticeships that are scalable.
