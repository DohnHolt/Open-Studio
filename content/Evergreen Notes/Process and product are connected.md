As young people get older and become more developmentally ready for more responsibility and more inclined to undertake things of greater depth and complexity and with more meaning to them as they seek to connect themselves with the social and ecological world around them they will undertake projects that they may need help managing. 

As facilitators it is our job to point out at this point that [[Products are slow moving verbs]] and that is it inevitable that the ongoing process of a project will change the outcome of the project, the only way to counter that is to recognise that they are tightly coupled and keep reflecting along the journey on how product is likely to be shifting due to process. 

ALC tools like intention-reflection cycles, kanban boards and the self managed learning approach of weekly reviews are all processes to help process talk to product. If not, projects are prone to drifting into composting as the easy reliance on uncoupled process drives them further away from the initial concept of product, until they don't seem to be tethered to anything meaningful for the young person, and they will unfortunately topple over, just like a penny farthing. 

Around the age of becoming a tween, and certainly when a teenager, young people need support thinking about how to frame projects around these two polarities. With the self managed learning approach we let the young people drive process but focus on helping them think about the product and reminding them of [[Tim's Bicycle]].
