If setting intentions is a vulnerable act then there requires a layer of trust between people in meetings where intentions and reflections are requested. If it is true that [[Meetings should be about connection]] then connection should be the foundation of the meeting and one of the ways that young people connect with each other the easiest is through play. Play leads to connection and it is through good, solid connection that trust and friendship is born and vulnerability available. Play then should be built into the core of the meeting. Games should be played whilst people are arriving and others are waiting or as an initial act to transition into the space. 

But also if we should constantly be thinking about constructing [[Parallel systems outside of meetings to facilitate capturing stated values]] can create systems that help to aid young people in expressing themselves in their particular ways, there is an argument that within a meeting there needs to be multiple ways of expressing intentions. 

Sometimes they might be parallel processes that are occurring together, such as encouraging the use of the whiteboard with sticky notes whilst allowing individual private notebooks as a place to collect intentions as well. However, they might also be different systems for different days. And games as vehicles for intention settings can be powerful because [[Meetings that are not considered can erode meeting culture]] - that is that if meetings are boring then that is the culture that will be created as [[Culture is the past expressed in the present moment]]. 

One of the challenges with this is that it has the potential to trivialise the notion of intention setting, for some this might be a bad thing, but for others that trivialisation can both demystify, and can limit the attention placed on the intention setter, making the act of vulnerability no longer that scary.

---
Games that I tried at The Garden:

The hole
- Throw the ball around the hole; if you drop it you go in and set an intention whilst in the hole. 
Duck duck goose
- Normal rules, whoever loses has to set an intention. 
Marco polo
- Normal rules except that when you get someone they have to say an intention out loud.
