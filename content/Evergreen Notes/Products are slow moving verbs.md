Process over product states that what is most beneficial for the young person is being allowed to focus on the process of creating art, for example, and not the finished product. This means the focus is on the creative process and the child's experience, and the uniqueness of each child's art rather than the finished piece. 

I don't think that these boundaried distinctions were ever meant to be either/or, however, there is a possibility in SDE to err too far to process over product. Ironically in the early years where this saying originates from the worry is that the easy thing to do is, without intention, fall into the trap of focusing too heavily on product. 

Alan Watts said once that nouns are slowing moving verbs. Products are indeed gerundives. When undertaking projects children are always going through the process of producing. When we say that we should focus on process we are really saying we want to focus on the child's agency and focus on the parts that are moving: process is always moving, whilst product is the final destination. 

But that belies a truth; that process is always shifting the product. Products really are slow moving verbs. Process is the big wheel constantly moving and the easy thing to focus on, but product is the small wheel which is always moving and they are always moving in tandem with each other a la [[Tim's Bicycle]].

Our job as facilitators is to keep both wheels moving and help the young person to see that they are in fact both moving and both talking to each other. As young people get older and the projects that they want to undertake become more complicated the more process has the possibility for affecting product. The more moving parts a project has the more opportunities for deviation from the initial end goal there is. Process is important, there is nothing wrong with deviation, but it is our job to highlight how [[Process and product are connected]] and how product actually can move.
