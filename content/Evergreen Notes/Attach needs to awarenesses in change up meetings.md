As described in [[Hope Wilder talks about sociocratic change up meetings]] attaching needs/values to each awareness helps keep the topic on point. It centres the awareness on the experience of the person who is raising it as an issue and helps to clarify the parameters of the discussion. By making the process of the meeting really clear the meetings can be concise and focused and you will end up with a [[Change up meeting culture that honours young people's needs]].

As an example teenager lost a badge near the football pitch and accused someone younger than them of losing it and then that incident became violent. The next week one of the awarenesses that was raised was that the teenagers were leaving their bags on the bench that was being used as a goal and could they please move them as the person did not want to hit them.

The awareness in that could be viewed as: your bags are in the way of our goal. The response from a teenager in this unstructured whole community conversation was

>*Sometimes when I eat on the bench and you come along and play football and ask me to leave I find that frustrating!

Here the conversation then became about whether the bench should be used for eating or should be used for football and how to navigate the dynamics of it's two uses. However, I suspect that the need, if it had been asked was related to the previous incident. I don't want to hit the bags was really a reflection on the aggression that was present previously and a fear that that might return if the football hit that person's bag. The need here is: I don't feel safe around you in communal spaces. 

The actual discussion was fruitful, lively and involved many people, but was it the discussion that the person raising the awareness actually wanted to have? Even though they were pretty engaged in the discussion themselves when it turned towards [[What is a bench-goal?]]
