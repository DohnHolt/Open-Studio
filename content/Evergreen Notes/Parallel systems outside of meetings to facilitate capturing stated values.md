Every meeting should have a process and every process should be designed to capture some value. Sometimes meetings might exist to capture values that are ephemeral or to enculture values and create culture. One belief I have of meetings for young people is that they work best when there is [[Psychological safety]] and to achieve that one of the aims should be to run meetings that make it clear to all that [[Meetings should be about connection]]

 Sometimes meeting processes are aiming to capture information from young people and in doing so one needs to think about the ways in which the process that we use can elicit that from young people in the best way. It's important to not become to focused on connection, playing games to create a sense of safety and connection between a small group, at the expense of gaining information; to facilitate means to constantly be thinking about the ways that we structure our processes to achieve as many aims and capture as much/many values as possible. 

How often have young people articulated thoughts one on one but then in a change up meeting where that awareness is then raised to the group they give so little of what they gave originally in a space that clearly has greater [[Psychological safety]]. [[There are plenty of ways of gaining information outside of community meeting processes]] and whenever that happens we should question whether the meeting was effective. Sometimes it might not be the process and it might be a new young person to out culture or they had a particular rough morning, but [[Culture is the past expressed in the present moment]] and if there is consistency then it should tell us that actually our meeting structure is not working. 

As well as trying to gain information from people, we might want to consider both push-pull systems. As well as trying to pull information out of people when a situation arises we might consider how we allow young people to push information towards us that we are not aware of. 

Instead of asking for awarenesses or project ideas in meetings and intention circles where people might not feel able to talk for a variety of reasons, say neurodivergence or introversion, we set up a suggestions box at The Garden that would allow people to write down these things in their own time, possibly anonymously, which we could then check as facilitators weekly. This idea also allowed us to capture another value of [[making literacy a valued practice within the community]].

When we made our [[First changes to The Garden meetings]] we aimed to reduce the daily planning of numerous games of tag that young people would spend ages debating on the time to do them, or the order in which to plan them, and then they would just ignore all that they had planned for twenty minutes and go ahead and play them when they felt like it. Someone would announce *"Come to the apple tree if you want to play..."*, and everyone who wanted to play would run over. The young people went around the planning process and came up with their own parallel system for arranging games of tag. The sad thing was that they still felt that they had to entertain the adult prescribed daily planning meeting as well, instead of just saying what a waste of time that is and running with the effective system they had designed.
