How do we build digital native education where children can [[Learning through observation]] online from experts and curate their own education? This is the question that Henrik Larsson asks in [Apprenticeship Online](https://escapingflatland.substack.com/p/apprenticeship-online).

Over a hundred years ago the Victorian school designers had a choice of apprenticeships, which were effective but not scalable, or school, which was scalable but not effective. We can now make another choice though and choose to create apprenticeships that are scalable.

This would be a situation where we can provide an [[open book approach to learning]] that transforms our current society with a black box of adulthood that children can not peer into. Because 'children need opportunities to observe skilled work, and through trial and error and lots of practice, attempt to replicate it'[^1]

In [[Indigineous pedagogies]] [[Apprenticeships]] are a very common way of learning some of the complex skills that are required for survival, or the ritual, magical or mystical epistemologies of the tribe. According to Christopher Alexander[^2] the best way to actually get to grips with learning something deeply is to help someone who really knows what they are doing, however, the greatest problem with apprenticeships historically has been that the master only has capacity for placing themselves in that relationship with only a handful of people at a time. However, the question of our time is how do we create situations in which we can make apprenticeships that are scalable and therefore resolve the tension that lies in providing the opportunity for learning without hindering the work of the expert who is being learnt from. 

How do we design workspaces where novices can enter at scale – and where they can easily observe the knowledge that guides experienced practitioners? And how can we do that without obstructing the work being done? [^3]
