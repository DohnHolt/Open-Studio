Psychological safety among young people is key for self-directed education. Sophie Christophy says you can't be truly self-directed unless you are being honest to who you really are, and you can't be honest about who you are to others around you unless you have the psychological safety to do so. 

In learning communities the culture has to be one in which psychological safety is fostered as much as possible. Adults should have meaningful relationships with young people. [[Meetings should be about connection]] and there should be a [[Change up meeting culture that honours young people's needs]]. Play should be a central part of an adult's vocabulary and meeting people where they are . Planning should be co-created; [[meeting people's plans where they are for planning]] and thinking about creating [[Parallel systems outside of meetings to facilitate capturing stated values]] are ways to be inclusive whilst having enough of a bones of a structure to work around for those who need structure. Structure is important to people and there are those who need clear structures and those should be considered, but that needs to be balanced off against those who work best spontaneously. [[Meetings that decrease agency decrease connection]] and parallel systems are one way of maintaining processes but allowing difference needs to surface and still be attended to. 

Processes need to be clear and well followed because consistency and certainty is a key tenant for creating psychological safety.
