How do we create a process in which we can raise awarenesses and talk about them but also say no to them in a way that feels good for everyone in the community? How can young people voice that the awareness feels pointless to them, especially when adults are often the ones who bring the pointless awareness, which are also often really complicated and/or broad topics?

When young people are forced to talk as a whole community in a round those who care talk but those who don't care don't talk, but moreover they also don't listen. And when a meeting is heading towards a resolution those who care will just leave if they can and allow one person to do the labour of bringing that resolution to it's conclusion. 

Sometimes the purpose of meetings feel like they are designed for adults to gain information, but [[Information from children is only information in a high trust environment]] and whilst some young people can reflect on that process as see the value in the moment they find it frustrating and a waste of time. [[Meetings should be about connection]] and [[Meetings that decrease agency decrease connection]] and if that is a consistent theme of meetings then [[Psychological safety]] will start to be eroded. 

When adults forget that this is a trade-off and focus purely on the instrumental benefits to them then this is childism, especially when they then claim that the young person's experience is not particularly valid because "good information" was gained from the meeting. Adults should remember that [[There are plenty of ways of gaining information outside of community meeting processes]] and that [[Parallel systems outside of meetings to facilitate capturing stated values]] is a powerful tool in a space with such a variety of neurodivergence and a breadth of age and maturity ranges.

If meeting culture is important to us then we need to be aware of how [[Meetings that are not considered can erode meeting culture]]. Not only do we have to consider if we are gaining information and if this is the right process for doing so, but how many people are interested, who is interested and as [[Young people are affected by each other's energy in meetings]] whether this combination will actually work in our favour. If young people are to be trusted to give us information we need to create the conditions for them to do so over time because [[Culture is the past expressed in the present moment]].
