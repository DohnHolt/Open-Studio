Studies have shown that when socioeconomic background and pre-existing academic ability is controlled college selectivity made no differences in income outcomes. A more recent refined study found that there may be a slight income difference (Ivy League students ranked on the 79th percentile versus the 77th percentile for state university graduates). But it is not just income, but life satisfaction and workplace engagement where there is a similar story of no difference with family life, finances, or jobs between graduates of private and public colleges, even without controlling for background factors. 

Attending a lower ranked college may be beneficial as a high-achieving student could stand out more prominently, potentially receiving greater attention and mentorship from professors, opportunities for advanced seminars or research, and stronger letters of recommendation.

Moreover, the experience of being a standout student can positively impact self-concept and may help foster greater motivation, achievement, and career aspirations. The confidence gained from excelling in a less competitive setting might ultimately drive long-term success.

These environmental factors, it seems, may help in [[Creating agency in young people]].
