In self-directed, consent based spaces children are given the freedom to express opinions and if the culture is working they should feel as comfortable as adults to do so. Therefore, the question one should always ask is this: is this a culture of a high trust environment? However, one must also remember that the child should never be taken at face value. In part because they are children and they communicate somewhat in riddles, and in part because these are spaces in which they are away from their primary care giver and there needs to be great psychological safety for them to open up fully. That we often get additional/contradictory messaging from parents demonstrates this fact.

Different parts of a setting can have varying degrees of trust. Each young person normally has one adult in the space that they feel most comfortable with and therefore a intimate conversation with that adult is inevitably going to be a much higher trust environment than with a different facilitator or in a meeting with the whole community.

The question facilitators should then be pondering on is how do we increase trust more broadly, for example if [[Meetings should be about connection]] are we actively building greater connection, or how do we leverage the parts of our system that we know are higher trust environments and seek to solicit opinions in those spaces, such as designing a project lab to recognise the challenges of creating psychological safety in reflections.

>Reflections need to happen individually for psychological safety and time to think environments to allow reflections to become deeper and more engaged, but there also needs to be a space for group reflection so that there can be accountability and collaboration on reflections to work out how we can do this as a team. Having reflected on things together is a precursor for creating a team and doing a project together, because if you trust they will reflect alongside you as the project progresses you can trust the project process. For adults I think they will take that risk, but for deschooling young people they need that trust up front much more. Even if the reflections have just been reflecting on each other's reflections, or reflecting on the maths sessions together. 

If it is true that [[Young people are affected by each other's energy in meetings]] just parsing a meeting for levels of engagement can be used as a proxy measure for whether this is a meeting is operating as a higher or lower trust environment.
