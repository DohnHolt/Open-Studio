One of the main advantages with working with computers for mathematics, especially graphically modelling geometry is that you can layer graphs on top of each other and then select which ones to show and which ones to hide. This is an example of [[Computers give instant mathematical feedback]]

However, you can also build up a complex number of graphs and instantly delete things and start again. 

Or more to the point you can manipulate a graph and then realise that it has not done what you want it to do and then delete the last few steps. On paper to remove a curve that you thought was correct but now realise was not would require a lot of rubbing out or a new piece of paper. In geogebra it is simply a click of a button or two.
