Under patriarchy boys are taught that the emotion most acceptable to access in conflict is anger, and that other emotions and feelings are to be suppressed in service to anger and rage. 

Boys need to be held differently because, as Bell Hooks says, boys are taught to access rage, but are also taught that in the presence of adults that that is not safe. That rage is the proper male emotion under patriarchy, but under patriarchy the father figure, then the mother figure (as upholder of patriarchy), then the boy is the natural order of who can and should access anger and rage. 

One way in which this might show up is [[As the number of boys increases the number of requests for conflict resolution decreases]]. In that instance boys need to be held firmly to the notion that conflict resolutions are there for a reason and that they must be adhered to in a timely fashion. 

They must also learn that actually intersectionality says that some of the affordances given to young people by facilitators might not be accessible when a boy is in conflict with a girl. Being given the opportunity to choose the time and place of a conflict resolution is not appropriate if a boy is using that to hold power over a girl; that should not be tolerated as it plays into the patriarchal patterns that might be underlying for the female in the space and in that conflict. 

Violence among boys needs to be tempered among boys in ways that it may not among girls, especially physical violence towards girls. But at the same time we have to consider that [[our acceptance of Noa's violence encultures Rowan into the patriarchy]].
