The process at The Garden is that during a meeting if there is a distraction then someone can highlight the distraction to the chair and then the chair can pull that person, or people, up and ask them not to carry on the distracting behaviour. 

In principle this sounds reasonable, however, there are some strong caveats. 

Firstly, the meeting length dictates the distractions and multiple continued distractions, occurring either in short duration and/or daily should be salient information that the meetings are too long. That is why a lot of ALC and learning communities only have meetings that are either optional or are timed and reasonably short. At The Garden meetings definitely were too long. Regularly when we started meetings could go on for 45 minutes almost every single morning. 

The response to this information (ongoing distractions) should be to shorten the meetings, not create a system where the information is penalised. However, that, it seems, was what was decided upon before Bess and I joined. 

Often the people making the distractions are the youngest in which case this is childism or have a particular neurodivergence that makes it hard for them to sit still quietly, this is ableist, just as it would in school. We were simply bringing in the behaviour modification system of schools but pretending to ourselves that we are doing it nicely and kindly. 

This brings to mind some of the criticisms that Alex Khost has of democratic schools still working on a fear based model in [[Self Directed Education is a Political Act]]. This request rests somewhat on a fear of being asked to leave the Garden, as meetings are compulsory and mandatory, is what makes people, or at the very least makes them think that they *must* try, to not be distracting. 

Labeling these distractions on our youngest and most divergent young people is problematic. Empowering young people to point them out in front of the whole group can be alienating and dehumanising and is problematic again. That they drive conversations about difference can be enlightening, but that they are structured to bring up those conversations by design, seems like we are profiting (through informative conversations) off the back of the "different" without their consent. 

[[Meetings should be about connection]] and we don't connect with young people when we call the out in a public forum, and we would do well do heed the caution [[there are ways in which transformative justice systems still rely on punitive systems]].
